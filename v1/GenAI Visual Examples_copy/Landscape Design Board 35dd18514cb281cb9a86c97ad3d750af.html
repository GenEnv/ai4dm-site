Landscape Design Board

Prompt

Generate a vertical A1 architecture presentation board for a street renovation and landscape design project. The board should include a clean site plan showing the redesigned street layout, sidewalks, planting zones, and street furniture, clearly integrated into the existing urban fabric. Include a photorealistic perspective rendering that illustrates the revitalized public realm with paving, greenery, pedestrians, and contextual buildings. Provide a detailed cross-section showing lane divisions, pedestrian walkways, bioswales, and stormwater management features. Annotate paving materials, planting types, and spatial dimensions. Add technical diagrams of stormwater systems such as permeable paving or swales, and a material palette showing surface textures, curbs, and tree pits. Use exploded views or zoomed-in vignettes to explain specific design elements like benches, lighting, or curb transitions. Apply a consistent graphic language with soft colors, clean linework, and minimalist fonts for clarity. The overall layout should feel professional, cohesive, and ready for design review or competition presentation.

Prompt

Generate a vertical A1 landscape architecture presentation board for a waterfront landscape design competition. The project should address both aesthetic and functional challenges of waterfront design, with a strong emphasis on resilience, climate adaptation, and anti-flood strategies.

The board should begin with a clear project title and concept statement at the top, introducing the vision for transforming the waterfront into a multifunctional, adaptive public space that balances ecology, recreation, and flood protection.

Include a masterplan in top-down view, showing the full layout of the waterfront landscape. Use realistic textures and clear zoning for green spaces, water edges, pedestrian promenades, wetlands, recreational nodes, and transitional floodable areas. Overlay minimal annotations or graphic icons for clarity.

In the central portion of the board, include resilience and anti-flood strategy diagrams, such as tidal charts, sea level rise projections, storm surge pathways, permeable surface ratios, bioswale locations, floodable zones, and green infrastructure typologies. Use layered, color-coded diagrams or axonometric cutaways to show how water is managed and redirected. Include a before-and-after flood scenario to visualize performance.

Add isometric diagrams that explain key spatial transformations—such as elevating pedestrian paths, layering flood terraces, or integrating hybrid grey-green infrastructure (e.g., sea walls with soft vegetated edges). These diagrams should highlight the spatial hierarchy and flood adaptability at a human scale.

Include sections and elevations showing how topography and edge conditions are designed to interact with varying water levels. These should show boardwalks, retaining walls, floodplains, and ecological buffers. Add people for scale and seasonal variation if relevant.

Place a bird’s-eye rendering toward the upper-mid section of the board to show the entire waterfront context with the city or natural surroundings. Use soft daylight, atmospheric depth, and realistic vegetation to evoke the project's immersive spatial experience.

Add smaller detail drawings such as paving sections, planting schemes, seating integration, lighting, or waterfront access points (docks, steps, ramps). Include material palettes and planting diagrams with native species chosen for water tolerance and habitat support.

Use a minimalist background, clean white margins, and a muted color palette with soft blues, greens, and warm earth tones. Diagrams should be clearly labeled but not overcrowded. Use modern sans-serif fonts, consistent line weights, and soft drop shadows to enhance clarity.

The final presentation board should communicate a compelling, resilient, and climate-adaptive waterfront design that is deeply integrated with its hydrological and urban context—suitable for high-level international competition review.

Prompt

Generate a vertical A1 landscape architecture presentation board for a resilient and sustainable park design competition. The board should clearly and beautifully present the full design process and concept, from large-scale strategy to detailed elements. The layout should follow a clean, professional visual hierarchy suitable for an international design competition.

At the top of the board, include a title section with the project name, a brief concept statement, and a subtle background graphic or site imprint. Below that, show a masterplan of the park in top-down view, with realistic vegetation textures, clearly defined paths, water features, planting zones, and park amenities. Overlay annotations or icons to highlight key spaces and programmatic zones.

In the middle section, include a series of conceptual and technical diagrams: site analysis (sun, wind, water flow), zoning, user flow, planting strategy, and ecological layers. Add resilient landscape strategies, such as floodable zones, bioswales, rain gardens, native planting, and habitat creation. Use color-coded diagram layers and simple visual icons to communicate systems thinking.

Integrate a bird’s-eye rendering showing how the park fits into its urban or natural context. Add sections or cutaway perspectives to illustrate spatial experience, topography, hydrology, or structural systems like retaining walls, decks, or shade structures.

Include material palettes, stormwater management diagrams, and planting typologies, using clean graphic keys and minimalist legends. If applicable, show phasing or adaptive design strategies to demonstrate long-term sustainability and climate resilience.

Use a light background, soft shadows, and consistent font families. Color palette should evoke nature—greens, earth tones, water blues—balanced with monochrome linework for legibility. Maintain visual clarity, generous margins, and a sense of breathing space between panels.

The final board should feel calm, detailed, and inspiring—ready to be submitted to a high-profile competition jury. It should tell the story of a resilient landscape that is ecologically sound, socially inclusive, and beautifully designed.

Prompt

Generate a vertical A1 architecture presentation board for an urban design competition. The board should visually communicate the full concept and design development of the project in a clear, professional, and aesthetically compelling format. Include a clean and labeled masterplan showing the overall urban layout with roads, public spaces, and key structures, integrated into the city context. Add a bird’s-eye perspective rendering that captures the relationship between buildings, landscape, streetscapes, and open space.

In the center portion of the board, place a series of clear diagrams illustrating site analysis, circulation flow, zoning strategy, and urban morphology. Add axonometric diagrams and exploded views to explain layers of public realm, mobility networks, green infrastructure, and building functions. Include building floor plans and sections that highlight relationships between interior and exterior, open space, human scale, and environmental design strategies.

At the bottom or side margins of the board, insert detailed drawings such as façade studies, streetscape sections, or paving and planting material palettes. Incorporate solar or environmental diagrams, and if applicable, resilience strategies like stormwater management or green corridors. Use a clear typographic layout, with consistent fonts and concise English annotations. Background should be white or light, with a harmonious balance of color accents used for emphasis in diagrams.

The overall composition should be refined, layered, and competition-ready—clearly articulating the urban vision from concept to detail, suitable for an international urban design competition.
