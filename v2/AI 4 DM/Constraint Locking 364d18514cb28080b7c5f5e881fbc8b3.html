Constraint Locking

As visual generation workflows are often time-intensive and rely on a meticulous back-and-forth process between the designer and AI, image distortion commonly emerges as ideas become increasingly refined. During this process, AI models may begin altering components that were never intended to change, even when modifications are directed toward only specific aspects of the image.

At advanced stages of refinement, these unintended changes can become particularly frustrating and difficult to reverse, as AI systems tend to retain and build upon characteristics from previous outputs. For this reason, constraint locking is critical during the iteration and refinement stages. By establishing clear visual and spatial constraints, designers can preserve successful aspects of an output while preventing important design elements from being unintentionally modified or lost throughout the iterative process.

Key Ideas:

  • State and/or Markup areas to preserve
  • Reuse (Input) previous references, renders, outputs
  • Use precise & strict language

Workflow Summary

Refer to the Iterative Prompting page to view an example of a full iterative workflow with constraint locking in each consecutive prompt.

As the iterative design process is most effective when changing one component at a time, once you are satisfied with a specific element, clearly prompt the AI to preserve that exact feature while modifying the next aspect of the design. This helps maintain consistency throughout iterations and provides greater control over the refinement process.

[Geometry]: do not alter any other elements, only change the form of the ———

[Material]: change —— and preserve [Geometry] including ———

[Furnishing/Details]: preserve the [Geometry], [Material] and ——— only add ———

[Atmosphere]: preserve the exact [Geometry], [Material], and [Furnishing/Details] and only change the atmosphere from —— to ——
