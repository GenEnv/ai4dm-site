Landscape Design Board

Prompt

Generate a vertical A1 architecture presentation board for a street renovation and landscape design project.

Site Plan

  • Show the redesigned street layout integrated into the existing urban context.
  • Include sidewalks, planting zones, street furniture, and roadway organization.
  • Use clear hierarchy, annotations, and clean urban design graphics.

Perspective Rendering

  • Include a photorealistic street-level rendering of the revitalized public realm.
  • Show paving, planting, pedestrians, and surrounding buildings.
  • Emphasize a vibrant, walkable, and comfortable streetscape.

Street Section

  • Provide a detailed cross-section showing lane divisions, sidewalks, planting zones, and bioswales.
  • Annotate key dimensions, materials, and landscape elements.
  • Clearly illustrate stormwater management strategies.

Technical Diagrams

  • Include diagrams explaining permeable paving, bioswales, and drainage systems.
  • Use exploded views or simplified axonometrics.
  • Clearly communicate environmental performance.

Material Palette & Details

  • Show paving materials, curbs, tree pits, and planting palettes.
  • Include zoomed-in vignettes of benches, lighting, and curb transitions.
  • Label materials and construction components clearly.

Presentation Style

  • Use soft colors, clean linework, and minimalist typography.
  • Maintain a balanced layout with generous white space.
  • Create a professional, competition-quality presentation board.

Prompt

Generate a image of a vertical A1 landscape architecture presentation board for a waterfront renewal and erosion mitigation project.

Site Plan

  • Create a photorealistic bird's-eye view master plan of the waterfront.
    Show planting strategies, shoreline stabilization measures, pathways, and new public realm additions.
    Use clear annotations, hierarchy, and labels to communicate key interventions.

Perspective Rendering

  • Include a photorealistic eye-level rendering from a waterfront sidewalk.
    Show native planting, shoreline improvements, public seating, and pedestrian activity.
    Emphasize the relationship between people, landscape, and water.

Waterfront Section

  • Create a detailed section through the shoreline.
    Illustrate vegetation root systems, erosion control measures, and added infrastructure.
    Clearly show how the design improves shoreline resilience and ecological performance.

Technical Diagrams

  • Include diagrams explaining ground surface health, drainage systems, and stormwater management.
    Visualize water movement, infiltration, and erosion mitigation strategies.
    Clearly communicate environmental performance and long-term resilience.

Material Palette

  • Showcase native plant species used throughout the project.
    Include materials for shoreline infrastructure, pathways, edging, and site furnishings.
    Provide concise labels and texture samples.

Presentation Style

  • Use a modern, minimal, and professional graphic language.
    Maintain a balanced layout with clean linework, subtle colors, and generous white space.
    Create a cohesive competition-quality presentation board suitable for design review.
