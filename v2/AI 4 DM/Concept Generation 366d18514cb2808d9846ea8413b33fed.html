Concept Generation

The Link From Loose Ideas to AI Generation

Large language models (LLMs) can support early concept development by translating simple inputs—such as objects, feelings, or precedents—into structured descriptions of spatial, atmospheric, and material qualities, helping clarify and expand initial design ideas.

LLMs are currently highly effective at interpreting casual and informal descriptions, meaning ideas do not need to be written in rigid or highly technical language. Even simple prompts can be understood and expanded upon, allowing the AI to extrapolate additional concepts, relationships, and design possibilities from minimal input.

LLMs for Design Ideas Brainstorm Examples

Object to Conceptual/Physical Qualities

Object: Chinese Lucky Knot

Ask: Translate the conceptual, cultural and physical qualities of a Chinese lucky knot into potential architectural design ideas in point form.

Output:

  • Interconnectedness & Unity
    Use overlapping circulation paths, interconnected programs, and visually linked spaces to create a sense of social and spatial connection.
  • Continuous Looping Geometry
    Explore flowing forms, looping circulation, or continuous roof and facade systems inspired by the endless woven nature of the knot.
  • Layering & Weaving
    Develop woven facade systems, interlaced structural elements, or layered spatial sequences that mimic the interlocking quality of the knot.
  • Cultural Symbolism
    Integrate themes of prosperity, harmony, protection, and luck into the atmosphere and experiential qualities of the architecture.
  • Structural Expression
    Use interdependent structural systems where components rely on one another, reflecting the tension and balance found in knot construction.
  • Thresholds & Gathering Spaces
    Create central communal nodes or intersection points where circulation overlaps, symbolizing unity and social interaction.
  • Material & Tectonic Inspiration
    Explore braided wood, woven metal screens, tensile systems, or patterned concrete that reinterpret the tactile qualities of knot-making.
  • Light & Shadow Patterns
    Use perforated or woven architectural elements to cast intricate shadows resembling the layered complexity of the knot.
  • Flexibility & Adaptability
    Design modular or adaptable spaces that can shift and reconnect similarly to the flexible nature of woven cords.
  • Spatial Narrative
    Organize the project as a journey of intertwining paths and unfolding spaces, reinforcing the symbolic idea of continuity and connection.

Exploring Existing Site Conditions

Site: John Hendry Park, Vancouver

Ask: In point form, analyze the social, environmental and historical conditions of Vancouver's John Hendry Park that could be potential design drivers.

Output:

  • Cultural Diversity of the Area

    The surrounding communities are highly multicultural, creating opportunities for inclusive public spaces that support varied social and cultural activities.

  • Connection to Trout Lake

    The lake acts as the ecological and visual centre of the park, influencing views, circulation, biodiversity, and recreational use.

  • Seasonal Environmental Changes

    The site experiences significant seasonal variation in rain, vegetation, light quality, and water levels, which can inform adaptable or climate-responsive design strategies.

  • Rainwater & Stormwater Management

    Vancouver’s wet climate and the park’s proximity to water make stormwater collection, filtration, and landscape integration important design considerations.

  • Pedestrian & Cycling Connectivity

    The park acts as a circulation node connecting surrounding residential areas, schools, and community amenities through walking and cycling routes.

  • Historical Relationship to Indigenous Land

    The site exists on the traditional territories of the xʷməθkʷəy̓əm (Musqueam), Sḵwx̱wú7mesh (Squamish), and səlilwətaɬ (Tsleil-Waututh) peoples, creating opportunities for Indigenous-informed design approaches and storytelling.

  • Recreation & Wellness Culture

    The park supports swimming, sports, walking, picnicking, and leisure, making health, wellness, and outdoor activity important spatial drivers.

  • Views & Topography

    Subtle changes in elevation and open views across the lake can inform spatial orientation, viewpoints, and experiential sequencing.

  • Climate Resilience Potential

    The site presents opportunities for flood mitigation, habitat enhancement, urban cooling, and resilient landscape strategies in response to climate change.

Programmatic & Spatial Iteration

Ask: If I want to design a community center, what are important programmatic and spatial considerations I should have and what are ways to iterate these?

Output:

  • Flexible Multi-Use Spaces
    Design spaces that can adapt to events, workshops, recreation, study, and community gatherings through movable partitions and modular layouts.
  • Circulation & Social Interaction
    Organize pathways and gathering nodes to encourage community interaction, visibility, and intuitive movement through the building.
  • Connection to Site & Outdoors
    Integrate courtyards, terraces, parks, and landscape connections to strengthen the relationship between indoor and outdoor community activity.
  • Accessibility & Inclusivity
    Ensure spaces are welcoming, easy to navigate, and usable for people of different ages, cultures, and physical abilities.
  • Program Relationships
    Carefully organize which spaces should connect, overlap, or remain separate to support functionality and community use patterns.
  • Atmosphere & Identity
    Use lighting, materiality, scale, and spatial qualities to create a strong sense of warmth, openness, and community belonging.

Summarize & Connect Key Design Drivers of Precedents

Precedents: The Wormhole Library by MAD Architects & Serpentine North Gallery by Zaha Hadid

Ask: For the free form architectural precedent: The Wormhole Library by MAD Architects & Serpentine North Gallery by Zaha Hadid, summarize their design qualities/drivers as well as how one could relate to another.

  • The Wormhole Library — MAD Architects

    Focuses on fluidity, immersion, and continuous spatial movement through soft organic forms and sculpted surfaces. The project blurs the boundary between structure, interior, and landscape, creating an almost cave-like spatial experience. Its key design drivers include exploration, curiosity, movement, and emotional atmosphere, with architecture acting as an experiential journey rather than a static object.

  • The Serpentine North Gallery — Zaha Hadid

    Emphasizes dynamic geometry, flowing circulation, and fragmented yet interconnected forms. The project explores movement through angular and curvilinear spatial relationships, creating a sense of motion and tension within the architecture. Key design drivers include fluidity, spatial continuity, visual dynamism, and the breaking of traditional rigid architectural boundaries.

  • Shared Design Qualities

    Both projects reject conventional rectilinear forms in favour of fluid, expressive geometries that guide movement and shape experience. They prioritize spatial flow, continuity, and immersive circulation over static composition. Architecture becomes experiential and sculptural, where surfaces, structure, and circulation merge together.

  • Relationship Between the Two

    Both precedents explore architecture as a continuous spatial journey. The Wormhole Library approaches this through soft organic carving and immersive enclosure, while the Serpentine North Gallery achieves it through dynamic fragmentation and flowing geometries. Together, they demonstrate how free-form architecture can create emotional, experiential, and movement-driven spaces that dissolve the distinction between form, structure, and circulation.

LLM to Image Generation (Prompting for Image)

After identifying key design drivers and core concepts, LLMs can be used to generate refined prompts for AI image generation. This allows design ideas to be visualized quickly, helping translate abstract concepts into architectural imagery for concept development, iteration, and visual comparison between different design directions.

Example Workflow

Ask: Based on the qualities of a Chinese Lucky Knot: (Insert Previous Points), create a prompt for Gemini to create 6 visual iterations of how these qualities could translate into a community center design.

Output Prompt:

Create 6 distinct architectural concept visualizations for a community center inspired by the qualities of a Chinese Lucky Knot. Each iteration should explore a unique spatial and formal interpretation of the knot’s qualities, including interconnectedness and unity, continuous looping geometry, layering and weaving, cultural symbolism, structural expression, gathering spaces, flexibility and adaptability, and spatial narrative.

The concepts should investigate how overlapping circulation paths, intersecting spaces, flowing forms, layered thresholds, and interwoven systems can shape community interaction and spatial experience. Explore ideas of continuity, connection, balance, and social gathering through different organizational strategies and architectural expressions. Some iterations may emphasize fluid and continuous movement, while others may focus on intersection points, central communal hubs, modular adaptability, or expressive structural systems.

Generate the concepts as contemporary architectural visualizations presented in a clean presentation layout. Each iteration should include one exterior perspective and one interior perspective that communicate the atmosphere, spatial qualities, and experiential intent of the design. Focus on conceptual architectural exploration rather than finalized building details, allowing for experimentation in form, circulation, scale, and spatial organization. Maintain realistic architectural proportions, environmental context, and people within the spaces to communicate community use and scale.
